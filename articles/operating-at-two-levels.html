Why operating at two levels made me a better professional

✍️
Patrick Le-Tran 2DEGREES & Front Row · 6 min read
Leadership Product

Most professionals live at one altitude. Either they're setting vision or they're shipping product. I've been doing both at the same time, and it's changed how I think about work entirely.

At 2DEGREES, I run the operation. That means setting direction, managing a team, and making sure the people around me are motivated and moving toward something bigger than any one of us. The north star is mine to define, and that's a responsibility I don't take lightly. Over the past two to three years of building this from the ground up, I've learned what it actually takes to align a group of people around an idea and get them to execute on it together.

At Front Row, I operate differently. My job there is execution: shipping things, solving specific problems, and digging into the details. It's the kind of work where you have to get comfortable not seeing the full picture. You're handed a task, and your job is to build it well.

One project that illustrates this: I was asked to build out a Buy Box strategy — a feature tied to whether a seller wins the add-to-cart placement on Amazon product pages. I didn't come up with the idea. My manager pointed me at the problem and said, go figure it out. But here's what surprised me: because I've spent years thinking about where a business is going, I couldn't help but bring that lens to the work. I kept asking myself what this needed to become, and what the version of this feature looked like that actually moves the needle long-term. That thinking made me a better contributor, even when my job was just to execute on someone else's directive.

Knowing when you're winning or losing, and understanding why, sharpens everything.

As a leader at 2DEGREES, I track what's working across our events, our team, our revenue. I understand the impact behind each decision. And that awareness bleeds into how I approach even the most tactical work at Front Row. I'm not just completing tasks. I'm thinking about what success actually looks like.

The flip side is equally true. The discipline of execution I've developed at Front Row — shipping fast, iterating on specifics, understanding the nitty gritty — makes me a sharper operator at 2DEGREES. I've stopped letting the big picture become an excuse to avoid the details. I know how to zoom out and zoom in, and I know which one a situation calls for.

Most people get one or the other. The rare thing is getting to train both muscles at the same time, and I wouldn't trade it.
