Distribution is king

👑
Patrick Le-Tran Product · 6 min read
Product GTM

A great product used to be enough. In a world where AI can help anyone build anything, it no longer is. The new competitive edge is distribution.

There's a theory I keep coming back to: if your product is good enough, it should sell itself. Word of mouth, organic growth, repeat customers. I believed this for a long time, and I still think it's mostly right. But the world has changed in a way that makes it incomplete.

With AI and modern building tools, the barrier to creating something is lower than it has ever been. That means more ideas, more products, more competition. Everyone has access to the same tools, so having a good product is now table stakes. The question that actually separates winners from everyone else is: how do you get it in front of people?

That is the central thesis I have come to believe through running 2DEGREES and working at Front Row: distribution is king.

The two-step framework

Make sure customers hear you
Distribution is the multiplier. A great product without reach stays invisible. Earning attention is the job.
Multiplier

What I learned at 2DEGREES: we throw events across New York City. We are not the only company doing this. What has set us apart is not just the experience we create, but how clearly and specifically we communicate what we are solving for our audience.

Going out in New York is genuinely hard. It is expensive, it can feel intimidating, and it is not always easy to find a night that feels worth it. We started making videos that named that problem directly and showed exactly how we solve it: good music, real community, great venues. When those videos started to take off, our ticket sales followed. Not because we suddenly had a better product — we already knew the product was strong from the feedback of our early crowd. The videos worked because they got our message in front of the right people at scale.

Our distribution channel happened to be social media. But distribution looks different depending on the business. It could be paid ads, SEO, PPC, email, partnerships, or word of mouth. The channel matters less than the clarity of your message and your ability to reach the right people with it.

At Front Row, I see the same principle playing out in ecommerce. Brands come to us because they have a real product and they want to grow, but growth does not happen automatically. It requires understanding how to place your product in front of buyers, how to write copy that speaks to actual problems, and how to manage your presence on a platform like Amazon where competition is constant and visibility is earned.

The through line in both worlds is the same. Start with a real problem your customer has. Build something that genuinely solves it. And then, just as importantly, make sure your customer actually hears that you solve it. That last step is distribution, and in 2025, it is the thing that separates products that scale from products that stall.

The best product does not always win. The best distributed product does.
